# 2026-10-17

## Done
- **F3: Max's spin clip** ([[Feel and Look Asset Specs]] M7), in a side session. Built in
  Blender on an R15-named armature whose bone axes match Roblox's joint axes, so the baked
  poses go straight into a `KeyframeSequence` ([[Blender to Roblox Animation]], new
  technique note; sources in `05 Assets/Animations/`). 18 frames at 30 fps, two front
  flips per loop (one per 0.3 s) in a tight somersault tuck (knees to chest, shins folded,
  arms hugging the shins), tightened twice after the first pass looked too loose.
- **The gun and the tux tails are hidden during the spin** (`LocalTransparencyModifier`,
  client-side), so nothing sticks out of the ball. The track plays at 2.5× (`SPIN_SPEED`), about 8
  flips a second, so the spin reads as a blur. The gun rides along: a client-only copy of `Character.Weapon` is welded to the
  UpperTorso at the new `SpinWeaponAttachment` while the real root-welded gun is hidden. (First pass stripped every
  WeldConstraint in the copy, so the rifle's parts floated off and looked static; only the
  root weld goes.)
  The flip pivots on the body's centroid (a translation baked
  into the Root pose at export) instead of the hip, and the client stops the clip on its
  own landing instead of waiting for the server's `Spinning` to clear.
- **`SpinLanded` remote:** the server used to end the spin only when its raycast saw the
  replicated root on the floor, 0.3–0.5 s after the client landed, so enemies at the
  landing spot popped late. The client now reports its landing and the server ends the
  spin on it (measured gap 0.07 s). Safe to trust: it can only shorten the player's own
  attack window.
- **[[AgentAnimationController]]** (new client controller) plays `Animations.SpinAnimation`
  while the player attribute `Spinning` is true and stops it on false, for every player.
  In Studio it plays the unpublished KeyframeSequence through a temporary id.
- Verified in a playtest: double jump → the track plays and loops, stops when `Spinning`
  clears, no console errors. Learned that the rig's joints are `AnimationConstraint`s.

- **The gun is in the hand now** (specs Q4 decided): `setWeapon` welds it to
  `RightHand.RightGripAttachment`, while `muzzlePosition` keeps the shot origin at the root
  offset, so the shot height is animation-proof. Verified: 3 shots in 0.7 s, tracers and
  flashes still at root +0.45; the spin copy still rides the torso.

- **Hold and Shoot clips** (specs M8 plus a gun hold): a two-handed rifle hold (always
  on, barrel 12° down, breathing bob) and a 5-frame recoil loop keyed on a new `Firing`
  attribute, sped to the weapon's `FireInterval`. Built in `Max_Rifle.blend` on a rig
  with the real R15 joints and a rifle stand-in on the hand; the left arm is solved by a
  two-bone IK onto the rifle. The grip convention changed to the tool one (barrel along
  the hand's −Y). Verified in play; the viewport capture was black this session, so the
  look wasn't screenshot-checked in Studio. Raised twice on request: the hold is now a
  chest-high ready stance (barrel 10° down) and the shot is a shoulder-level straight-arm
  aim (hand at the shoulder joint height, barrel level, recoil to +8°).
- **The original's animations, converted** (cloud session): all 211 agent and Wild West
  enemy clips turned onto our R15 rig, as Blender actions (`Original_Clips_*.blend`) and
  as Studio files (`05 Assets/Animations/Studio/Orig_*.rbxm`). Poses checked against
  Blender and the files against a Roblox file reader; not yet played in Studio
  ([[Original Animation Decoder]]).
