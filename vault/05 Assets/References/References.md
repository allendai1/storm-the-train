## 4. Side-on 3D readability: 2.5D side-scrollers built from 3D models

| Reference | Link | What to take from it |
|---|---|---|
| *Donkey Kong Country: Tropical Freeze* (Retro Studios), Nintendo page | https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/donkey-kong-country-tropical-freeze-switch/ | **Background silhouette levels** (sunset stages): the gameplay plane gets crushed to near-black shapes against a bright sky and still reads. That proves silhouettes alone carry the play. It also shows chunky 3D characters with a big head, big hands and a clear facing direction. |
| *Super Mario Bros. Wonder*, official site | https://supermariobroswonder.nintendo.com/ | **The modern benchmark for 3D models on a 2D plane:** saturated actors on calmer, pastel backgrounds, an exaggerated anticipation pose on every action, and faces turned about 3/4 to the camera even when moving sideways. |
| *Kirby's Return to Dream Land Deluxe*, official site | https://kirbysreturntodreamlanddeluxe.nintendo.com/ | **4-player co-op on one screen:** each player gets a distinct body colour and silhouette, and big, simple shapes stay readable through crowded VFX. That's the same problem as our 4 agents. |
| *Trine 4: The Nightmare Prince* (Frozenbyte), Steam page | https://store.steampowered.com/app/690640/Trine_4_The_Nightmare_Prince/ | **The caution:** lush 2.5D depth. Watch how the gameplay surfaces get a brighter rim or edge than the decoration behind them. Busy, painterly backgrounds can bury characters, so don't copy the background density. |
| Game Developer: "Art design deep dive: giving back colors to cryptic worlds in *Dead Cells*" | https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-giving-back-colors-to-cryptic-worlds-in-i-dead-cells-i- | **An explicit contrast ladder:** background lowest, then gameplay surfaces, then enemies and projectiles highest in saturation and contrast. *Dead Cells* builds its characters from 3D models, so the lesson carries over. |
| Game Developer: "Designing for difficulty: readability in ARPGs" | https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/designing-for-difficulty-readability-in-arpgs | Readability = **telegraph** (before: big gesture, colour code, sound) + **expectation** (after: a consistent result). It backs up the Art Direction rule that the wind-up pose *is* the telegraph. |
| Game Developer: "How *Cuphead* nailed the look and feel of classic 1930s cartoons" | https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/how-i-cuphead-i-nailed-the-look-and-feel-of-classic-1930s-cartoons | **A run-and-gun boss benchmark:** bosses designed around hitboxes first, with big, rubbery wind-ups and readable projectiles over painted backgrounds. Its "cartoon slapstick, never mean" tone fits ours. |
| Game Developer: "The evolutionary process of building *Rayman Origins*" | https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-evolutionary-process-of-building-i-rayman-origins-i- | Ancel: "**2D shows every collision, mistake and control error.**" That's a reminder that side-on exposes bad silhouettes, so keep shapes clean and keep the collision surfaces visually obvious. |

**What makes silhouettes read side-on (summary of the above):**
- Identity comes from **what sticks out** (hats, weapons, capes, bags), not from what's printed on the body.
- Exaggerate in the **horizontal axis**: long weapons, a big hat brim, a cape that trails behind. Depth (Z) detail is invisible from the side.
- Keep a **gap of negative space** between the arms and the torso and between the legs, so poses read as poses.
- Cheat the **3/4 turn**: the body runs sideways, but the face and chest angle toward the camera, so the expressions and chest detail land.
- **Value contrast against the backdrop** matters more than hue. Do a squint or greyscale check.

## Style sheet: Train Mayhem, Wild West

> Built on [[Art Direction]] (planned, accepted 2026-10-13). Items marked **[AD]** restate
> decided direction. Items marked **[ref]** come from the board above and are
> **proposals** for the developer to confirm.

**Look in one line:** a Saturday-morning cartoon in 3D. Chunky, rounded toon actors pop off
a calm, dusty, graphic desert that slides past a stationary train.

### Shapes
- **[AD]** Standard R15 bodies. Identity comes from **hats, props, capes and weapons**. Every actor passes the flat-black **side silhouette test**.
- **[ref]** Push shapes **horizontally**: hat brims, rifles, tux tails, capes and Lasso Lucy's rope. Detail in depth is wasted on a side-on camera (TF2, DKC).
- **[ref]** Use **big–medium–small** shape rhythm on every character. Example for Max: big wedge torso, medium square head, small feet (*Incredibles*, *Spies in Disguise* "martini glass"). Keep legs thick enough not to strobe in fast motion.
- **[ref]** Make enemy types differ in **body mass and height** first, not colour: thin Rodeo Kid, round Big Bart, tiny Runts. Graded heights, as with the Daltons, make crowds varied for free.
- **[ref]** Train cars carry a **side-on signature each**: engine = balloon stack plus cowcatcher wedge; boxcar = solid block plus catwalk; cattle car = see-through slats; tanker = cylinder; flatbed = open deck; caboose = cupola. Exaggerate stacks, wheels and cupolas by about 1.2–1.5×.
- **[ref]** Use **rounded bevels** on every hard edge (car corners, crates, rocks). No razor edges: soft corners catch a highlight and read as "toy".

### Outline and finish
- **[AD]** Mostly **SmoothPlastic** with base colours, plus a few soft, low-contrast MaterialVariants. `SurfaceAppearance` only for hero meshes, with **hand-painted gradients**.
- **[ref]** Separate shapes with **value and rim contrast**, not ink lines (TF2). Paint a light-to-warm gradient top-down on hero meshes, with a slightly lighter top edge so the figure reads against the sky.
- **[ref, open]** No universal black outline. Roblox `Highlight` has a small per-scene limit (about 31), so it can't cover crowds, and inverted-hull outlines double the triangle cost. Possible exceptions: the **enemy projectiles' dark outline** **[AD]** and a short outline on the active telegraphing enemy. Test both in Studio.
- **[ref]** Use **simple, chunky, low-frequency** texture patterns: board lines, rivets as a few big dots, a cloth weave as soft stripes. If it's not readable at phone size, delete it.

### Palette by layer
- **[AD] Foreground** (rare): **dark, low-detail** silhouettes such as telegraph poles, saguaros and rocks, on screen only briefly. Avoid partial transparency here (a mobile cost, per Roblox docs).
- **[AD] Gameplay plane:** the **most saturated and highest contrast**. Freight-car red `#B04A33`, wood `#7C5A3A`, iron `#3B3B42`. Actors go on top of that at full saturation.
- **[AD] Background:** **lighter and desaturated**, getting paler and bluer with distance. Mesas `#D9A27C`, sand `#E8CBA2`, sage `#A9B38A`, sky `#8EC3E6` → `#F6DDB4`.
- **[ref]** Take the **colour ratio** from *Rango* and *Mario Wonder*: about 80% calm earth tones, 20% saturated accents, and every accent is something you can interact with.
- **[ref]** Make the background **graphic, not textured** (Noble, Dixon, Ed Mell). Use 3–4 flat value steps per mesa, faceted planes lit by one sun, and big flat-bottomed cloud cards on the slowest layer.
- **[AD]** Reserved colours stay reserved: danger orange-red `#FF4A1C`/`#FF5A2A`, player colours P1–P4, and gang **bandana red** `#D7322F` on every bandit. **[ref]** Don't use these hues anywhere in the backdrop, to keep a clean colour language.

### Lighting mood
- **[AD]** A **warm afternoon sun** as the single key light, from slightly in front of the gameplay plane so faces toward the camera are lit. **Cool ambient**. `Atmosphere` for distance fade, light `Bloom` and a gentle saturation and contrast boost. No heavy depth of field.
- **[ref]** Grade the fog so the **far layers lean blue-lavender** and the **near layers stay warm**. That's the cheapest depth cue (Firewatch, Monument Valley panoramas).
- **[ref]** For set-pieces such as a canyon entry, a sunset leg or the boss approach, consider a **silhouette moment** like DKC's: background bright, gameplay plane nearly dark. It only works if the silhouettes already pass §1. Treat it as a later polish beat.
- **[AD, open]** ShadowMap vs Future waits for a mobile test.

### Readability rules (checklist for every asset)
1. **Silhouette test:** a flat black side view is still identifiable. **[AD]**
2. **Squint test:** in greyscale, the gameplay plane is clearly the highest-contrast band, and actors separate from the car roofs. **[ref: Dead Cells contrast ladder]**
3. **Telegraph = pose + colour + shape:** a wind-up pose, an orange-red glow and a "!" pop. Never colour alone. **[AD]**
4. **Face the camera:** bodies run side-on, but heads and chests cheat about 20–30° toward the camera so faces and chest props read. **[ref: Mario Wonder, Kirby]**
5. **Negative space** around the arms and between the legs in key poses. **[ref]**
6. **Projectiles:** player shots are bright, warm and unoutlined. Enemy shots are orange-red with a dark outline and a smoky trail. Make them bigger than "realistic". **[AD; ref: Nijman "bigger bullets"]**
7. **Background never competes:** no saturated or high-contrast detail behind the gameplay plane, and no reserved hues. **[AD; ref: Trine caution]**

### Feel (VFX and camera support for the art)
- **[AD]** No blood. Hits make white flash sparks. Knock-outs make dust puffs, stars and splinters. Explosions are round toon puffs, with an orange-yellow core and then grey smoke balls.
- **[ref]** A **hit flash** (a 1–2 frame white tint) on every damage tick. A **short hit-stop** (about 3–6 frames) only on knock-outs, boss staggers and gadget hits (Sakurai).
- **[ref]** **Trauma-based camera shake:** shake = trauma², Perlin noise, fast decay. Keep it small and mostly rotational on the side-on camera, and **cap it** so 4 players' explosions can't stack into a mess (Eiserloh, "Don't Juice It").
- **[ref]** **Permanence:** ragdolled bandits, dropped hats and splinters stay on the roof for a moment before fading **[AD]**. It's a big part of why hits feel heavy (Nijman).
- **[ref]** Squash and stretch through **poses and UI tweens**, since R15 parts don't deform **[AD]**. Use overshoot on landings, coin pops and combo text.

### Budget bar
- **[AD]** Use the §11 triangle and texture budgets, and test on a **real mid-range Android phone** (Roblox docs: Android is about 65% of players). Benchmark the look and frame rate against *Sonic Speed Simulator* (custom toon meshes), *Adopt Me!* (budget floor) and *Jailbreak* and *Dead Rails* (trains).
